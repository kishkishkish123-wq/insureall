Coverage

What life insurance actually covers.

A death benefit is only the starting point. Here's what it's really for.

  • Income replacement

    Ensures your family maintains their standard of living if your income disappears.

  • Mortgage & debt payoff

    Prevents loved ones from losing the family home or inheriting your liabilities.

  • Final expenses

    Covers funeral costs, medical bills, and estate settlement fees immediately.

  • Children's future

    Earmarked funds for education, so your children's plans stay intact.

ⓘ

When this might not be necessary

If no one depends on your income — no children, no partner relying on you financially, no co-signed debt — a large life insurance policy usually isn't urgent. A small final-expense policy may still be worth it to avoid burdening family with funeral costs.

FAQ

Common questions.

A common approach: add up your outstanding debt, income you'd want replaced (annual income × years needed), remaining mortgage balance, and future education costs. That total is a reasonable coverage target — not a generic multiplier of your salary.

Term life covers you for a fixed period (10, 20, or 30 years) at a lower cost. Permanent life (whole or universal) covers you for life and builds cash value, but costs significantly more. Most people with temporary needs — like a mortgage or young kids — are better served by term.

Many applicants under 50 in good health qualify for accelerated underwriting using health records and prescription history, without a physical exam. Older applicants or larger coverage amounts may still require one.

Term policies typically include a conversion option, letting you switch to permanent coverage without a new medical exam, usually within the first 10-20 years. You can also apply for additional term coverage separately as your needs change.
