Life Insurance

How much life insurance do you actually need?

The most common life insurance mistake isn't buying the wrong type of policy — it's buying the wrong amount. Here's how to calculate a number that actually protects your family.

7 min read·July 2026

The multiplier method (and its limits)

Most advisors start with a simple rule: 10 to 15 times your annual income. If you earn $70,000 a year, that suggests $700,000 to $1,050,000 in coverage. This is a reasonable starting point, but it ignores your actual financial picture — existing debt, dependents' ages, and other assets you may already have.

A more precise approach: DIME

A more accurate method adds up four categories. Debt: everything you owe beyond your mortgage — credit cards, loans, medical debt. Income: your income multiplied by the number of years your family would need replacement support, typically until your youngest child is financially independent. Mortgage: your remaining mortgage balance, so your family isn't forced to sell the home. Education: estimated future tuition costs for each child, adjusted for inflation.

What most people get wrong

The biggest error is treating life insurance as a fixed, one-time decision. Your coverage needs peak in your 30s and 40s — when your mortgage is largest and your children are youngest — then shrink as debt is paid down and children become independent. A term policy that doesn't adjust with your life stage often leaves you over-insured late in the term and under-insured early on.

Group life insurance isn't enough

Many people assume the life insurance offered through their employer is sufficient. It typically covers just 1 to 2 times your salary — a fraction of what the DIME calculation recommends. It's also not portable: if you leave your job, that coverage usually disappears with it.
