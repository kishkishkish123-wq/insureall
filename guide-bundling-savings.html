Money & Savings

Does bundling insurance actually save you money?

Multi-policy discounts are one of the most consistently underused ways to lower your total insurance spend. Here's how the math actually works, and when bundling isn't the right call.

6 min read·July 2026

Why insurers discount bundles

Acquiring a new customer costs an insurer money — underwriting, onboarding, marketing. When an existing customer adds a second policy, that cost is far lower, and insurers pass part of the savings back to you. It's also a retention play: policyholders with multiple bundled products are statistically far less likely to switch providers.

The typical savings range

Bundling two policies together commonly saves 15% to 20%; three or more can push savings toward 25% to 30%, depending on the categories combined. Auto + home is the most common and often the most discounted pairing, since both are high-frequency purchase categories for insurers.

When bundling isn't worth it

Bundling only saves money if both individual policies are competitively priced to begin with. If one insurer's standalone rate for, say, home insurance is notably higher than a specialist competitor, the bundle discount may not close that gap. It's worth comparing your bundled total against the best individual rates you could get elsewhere before committing.

How to maximize your bundle

Start with your two highest-value policies — often auto and home, or health and life. Add coverage types incrementally rather than all at once, and re-quote your bundle annually; multi-policy discounts sometimes grow as you add tenure and claims-free years to your account.
