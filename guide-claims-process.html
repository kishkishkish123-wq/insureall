What actually happens after you file a claim

Filing a claim is often the moment an insurance company either earns your trust or loses it permanently. Here's a realistic walkthrough of what happens behind the scenes.

8 min read·July 2026

The first 24 hours

When a claim is filed, it's logged and assigned a claims handler — at InsureAll, this is a single named concierge rather than a rotating call-center queue. Basic details are verified against your policy: is the incident type covered, is the policy active, and does the timeline match your coverage dates.

Documentation and assessment

This stage varies most by claim type. A auto claim might require photos and a repair estimate; a home claim may need an in-person or virtual inspection; a health claim typically needs an itemized bill from the provider. The single biggest cause of claim delays industry-wide is incomplete documentation submitted upfront — providing everything requested in the first submission meaningfully speeds up resolution.

Why claims get denied

Most denials fall into a few categories: the incident falls outside the policy's covered perils, the policy had lapsed for non-payment, the loss exceeds a stated exclusion (like flood damage on a standard home policy), or there's a material misrepresentation on the original application. A denial is not always final — nearly every insurer, InsureAll included, offers an internal appeal and independent review process.

What 'fast' actually looks like

Simple, well-documented claims — a minor auto collision with clear fault, for example — can resolve in 48 to 72 hours. Complex claims involving injury, large losses, or disputed liability typically take several weeks regardless of insurer, because they require more extensive verification. Industry-wide average settlement time sits around 30 to 45 days; InsureAll's named-concierge model brings that down to an average of 7 days for most claim types.
